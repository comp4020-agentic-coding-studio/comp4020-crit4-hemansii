# Crit 4 — An instrument

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Realising I could direct work I couldn't have written myself.

The xylophone was my idea, and so was recording what you play and playing it
back, so two people could hear how differently they play the same instrument.
But I don't know music theory. Claude picked the scale — two octaves of C
major pentatonic — and explained why it mattered: any combination of those
notes sounds fine together, so there is no wrong note to hit. I couldn't have
chosen that, and I wouldn't have known there was a choice to make.

What I could do was listen and look. The brief says an agent can build a synth
but can't hear the result, and that turned out to be exactly the split.
Almost every fix this week started with me saying something was off without
knowing why: hitting the top and bottom of a bar didn't sound different
enough, the gold pipes under the bars looked wrong, the mallet cursor looked
like a magnifying glass and then like a matchstick, and "how hard it sounds"
read strangely. Each time, Claude went and found the actual cause — the pipes
were attached to the wrong part of the layout, the cursor was drawn as an
outlined ring, the letters failed a contrast standard I had never heard of.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a developer?

I used to assume I had to fully understand something before I could ask for
it, and that made me hesitant. I still want to learn the theory — I would
rather understand the sound than only judge it. But I have stopped thinking
that not knowing it disqualifies me from deciding whether the work is good.
